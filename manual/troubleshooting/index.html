    Troubleshooting¶

    Problem Likely cause What to do
    Nothing happens when the pack is plugged in Pack switch OFF; a newly fitted pack, or a pack just switched back ON, that has not yet seen USB-C power; pack below the 6.36 V turn-on; or the pack's protection has switched it off after a deep discharge Switch the pack ON. Plug in the USB-C adapter: it wakes the board and the pack, and charges the pack. After a ship-mode shutdown, press SW1.
    Board runs from the adapter but never from the battery Pack switched off, a cell missing or reversed, pack over-discharged, or the pack's temperature cut-off is active Unplug the adapter and the pack. Check the pack switch and each cell's orientation. Let a hot pack cool, then reconnect and charge for at least 30 minutes.
    PWR LED off on battery, but on with the adapter Cells are flat, or the pack's over-discharge protection has switched it off Charge with the adapter connected for at least 30 minutes. If it still will not run on battery, measure each cell; replace any cell below 2.0 V.
    Battery will not charge at all A cell is above 4.25 V, so the pack blocks charging; or a cell is dead Measure each cell. Replace a dead or mismatched cell with a new cell matching the other.
    Pack switches off by itself Temperature cut-off (about 60 °C) Let it cool, then connect the adapter to bring it back. Improve ventilation round the pack, keep it out of the sun and away from the board's hot parts.
    CHG LED blinking, battery not charging Charge fault, such as the charge safety timer expiring or a cell over-voltage Unplug and reconnect the adapter. If it continues, check each cell's voltage and replace mismatched cells.
    i2cdetect -y 1 does not show 6b or 28 I²C not enabled, or the board is not seated correctly Enable I²C with sudo raspi-config and reboot. Check the header is not offset by a pin.
    ups-hat log shows No such file or directory: /dev/i2c-1 I²C is not enabled Enable I²C with sudo raspi-config and reboot.
    ups-hat log shows Remote I/O error The board was off, or another device is holding the bus Check the PWR LED is on. Remove other I²C HATs and try again.
    Battery current reads 0 A, or no readings The ups-hat service is not running Start it with sudo systemctl enable --now ups-hat, or install it (see Raspberry Pi configuration).
    Charging at 8.4 V or 1 A after unplugging the pack Disconnecting the pack resets the charger to its defaults Normal. The service re-applies its settings within a few seconds.
    Charge % wrong after fitting new cells The estimate re-calibrates at the next full charge Charge fully once with the adapter connected, and check that UPS_HAT_CELLS_PARALLEL and UPS_HAT_CELL_MAH in the service match your pack.
    Runtime much shorter than expected Cells are mismatched or worn, so the weaker cell reaches its cut-off first Replace all cells together with new matched cells.
    Pi 5 shows a low-power or USB current warning The Pi cannot detect a 5 A supply through the header Add usb_max_current_enable=1 and PSU_MAX_CURRENT=5000 (see Raspberry Pi configuration).
    Pi reboots or freezes under heavy load on battery Cells nearly empty or not rated for the load current Charge the cells. Use cells with a higher discharge rating, or move to the QUAD pack.
    Charging is slow, or the battery drains while on mains Adapter is 5 V only or under 27 W Use a USB-C PD adapter of 27 W or more with 9 V or 15 V output.
    Board stays on after sudo poweroff The adapter is connected (by design), or the shutdown hook is not installed On mains, unplug the adapter. Otherwise install the shutdown hook (see Raspberry Pi configuration).
    Pi does not restart when mains returns The Pi halted on mains and is waiting for its power button Press the Pi's power button, or disconnect and reconnect the adapter.

    If the problem continues, note the board's revision and the output of i2cdetect -y 1 and of journalctl -u ups-hat -n 50 before contacting support.
